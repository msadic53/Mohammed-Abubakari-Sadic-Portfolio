I'm Mohammed Abubakari Sadic, an MSc IT student at Carnegie Mellon University Africa concentrating in Cybersecurity and Applied Machine Learning. My work lives at the intersection of threat detection, predictive modeling, and data-driven decision-making.

Technically, I work across the full ML pipeline — from data engineering and statistical inference through to model deployment and explainability using SHAP. I approach security problems with the same rigor: understanding adversarial behavior through data, building resilient systems, and communicating risk clearly.

Before CMU Africa, I spent three years as a Teaching Fellow with Lead For Ghana — bringing ICT and STEM education to underserved communities across Tamale. That experience grounds everything I build: technology should serve people.

I'm deeply passionate about community development and educational technology. I believe EdTech is one of the most powerful levers for equity in Africa — and I'm actively building at that intersection. My current research at CMU Africa investigates how learning analytics can be redesigned for African higher education contexts, accounting for infrastructure gaps, cultural norms, and data privacy realities that Global North tools routinely ignore.

Live Research · CMU Africa · 2026
Learning Analytics for African Higher Education: A Student Performance Dashboard for LMS

Analysing 830,000+ Moodle clickstream events from the University of KwaZulu-Natal (ISTN101, 2021) to identify which engagement signals predict student success in African university contexts — and designing a privacy-conscious dashboard framework built for African infrastructure and cultural realities. Co-authored with Nthabiseng Thema and Gentille Uwera; supervised by Dr. Thomas McManus.

→ System nav r = 0.605 — strongest completion predictor
→ k-means (k=3): Low tier = 48.6% of 742 students, 15% completion
→ Content access (4.1×) & forum (5.4×) differentiate tiers more than quiz volume
